What the theme is built around

Most fashion themes assume the product is the unit of shopping. Pet clothing works differently: people choose an outfit for an occasion, then discover the individual pieces inside it. The theme leans into that.

  • Look-first browsing. The runway section steps through complete outfits with the keyboard, the scroll wheel or a drag. Each look carries its own scene — a tea party, a rainy day walk — and its own three pieces.
  • Saving without an account. Customers can mark looks as saved and come back to them on the same device. Nothing is stored on your server and no login is required.
  • Shopify owns the data. Prices, variants, stock, images and tax behaviour all come from your products. The theme has no duplicate product settings to keep in sync, so editing a product in admin changes the storefront immediately.
  • Complete before you add content. Sections that need imagery fall back to bundled photographs, and the two product sections can render demo pieces. A fresh install looks finished, which makes it easier to judge the design before you upload anything.

What it needs from your store

The theme runs on any Shopify plan that supports Online Store 2.0, which is every plan today. Beyond that it expects:

  • Products with images and prices — the grids and the product page read from them directly.
  • At least one navigation menu for the header. Menus are created in Online Store → Navigation, not inside the theme.
  • Collections if you want the look grids driven by merchandising rather than a hand-picked product list.
  • More than one country or language before the footer selectors appear — a single-country store shows no selector at all, by design.
Not included

No apps, no review widgets, no popup builders, no page builder. Every element you see is part of the theme. If you need reviews or upsells, install those apps separately; the theme does not reserve space for them.

Demo content, and replacing it

Two home-page sections — Featured looks and Product grid — have a source setting with two useful values: demo and collection (or a hand-picked product list). Out of the box both are set to demo, which renders bundled sample pieces so the layout can be evaluated before your catalogue exists.

When you are ready to sell, switch source to a collection or a product list. The demo pieces disappear completely — they are not products, they cannot be added to a cart, and they never appear in search or on collection pages.

Before you publish

Leaving a section on demo in a live store means customers see items they cannot buy. Walk the home page once with the theme editor open and confirm every grid is pointing at real products.
